1. What Is This App?

This is our internal tool for tracking everything related to the Community-Based Sensor Network. It replaces the old Salesforce workflow and puts everything in one place: sensors, the communities they're deployed in, contacts at each site, service tickets, collocations, and audits.

Sensor Management

Track every sensor's location, status, install date, and full history. Move sensors between communities with automatic logging.

Service Tickets

Track repairs and calibrations with QuantAQ from opening through shipping, repair, progress notes, and return.

Collocations and Audits

Track progress, upload data, run regression analysis, and generate formatted reports.

Communities & Contacts

Each community's sensors, contacts, communications, and files, with parent/child communities and optional tags for sub-networks such as the Interior Wildfire Network or Muni of Anchorage Network.

2. Getting Started: Signing In & Finding Your Way

Go to adec-projects.github.io/sensor-network-tracker. You can only create an account once an administrator has invited your email address (you'll get an email with the link). Sign up with that same email.

Signing In, Passwords & Your Authenticator

Signing in takes your password and a 6-digit code from an authenticator app on your phone (Microsoft Authenticator, Google Authenticator, or similar). The first time you sign in, the app walks you through setting up the authenticator. If you can't scan the QR code, click Can't scan? Enter a key instead and type the key into your authenticator app.

  • Change your password: click the gear icon next to your name at the top of the sidebar to open Settings. Under User Management, find your email (marked "(you)") and click Reset password. Enter your current password and your new one twice. You don't need your authenticator code. Changing it signs you out on your other devices.
  • Forgot your password: click Forgot password? on the sign-in page and contact an administrator. They'll give you a temporary password by phone or Teams. Sign in with it and your 6-digit code, and you'll be asked to choose a new password.
  • Switching to a new phone: while you can still sign in, open Settings and click Reset MFA on your own row. Confirm with your password. You'll be signed out, and when you sign in again you'll set up your authenticator on the new phone.
  • Lost your phone and can't sign in: on the code screen, click Can't access your authenticator app? to see who the administrators are. An administrator resets your authenticator, and you set it up again at your next sign-in. Your account and data aren't affected.
  • Passwords need at least 8 characters, with a letter, a number, and a symbol.
  • Automatic sign-out: for security, you're signed out after an hour of inactivity.

The Layout

The sidebar on the left has your name, the gear (Settings), and Sign out at the top, then:

  • Dashboard: a quick overview of the whole network (see Section 3).
  • All Sensors: every sensor, with search and filters. The ▼ arrow filters by type (Community Pod, Audit & Permanent Pods, Not Assigned). Underneath are Audits, Collocations, and Quant Service Tickets, each with a count.
  • Communities: all communities, alphabetical, with sub-communities nested under their parent. The ▼ arrow filters by tag. Communities you've pinned are listed here too.
  • Contacts: everyone involved in the network, grouped by community.
  • User Guide (this page) is at the bottom of the sidebar, along with the Setup and Sandbox toggles for admins.

Search, Tabs & Pins

  • Search bar: the box at the top of every page ("Search sensors, communities, contacts...") finds a sensor by ID or SOA tag, a community by name, or a contact by name, organization, or email. Click a result to open it.
  • Open tabs: records you open stay as tabs across the top, so you can switch between them. ← Back returns to the previous page.
  • Pins: open a community you use often and click ☆ Pin. It appears under Communities in the sidebar. Pins are saved in your browser, so they won't follow you to another computer.

3. Dashboard

The Dashboard is the landing page. It's a quick-glance overview of the network and a starting point for logging. The buttons at the top are + New Log, + Service Ticket, and + Add Sensor.

Summary tiles

Tiles show total Sensors, how many are Online, the number of Communities, open Service Tickets, and Active Audits. Click any tile to jump to that list.

Needs-attention cards

  • Sensors needing attention: pods carrying an issue status (PM, gaseous, SD card, needs repair, lost connection, or firmware) or an open service ticket. It shows the top few, with a "+ X more" link that opens the sensor list filtered to the flagged pods. SD card issues sort last since they're lower priority.
  • Open service tickets: tickets currently in progress with Quant.
  • Audits in progress: audits that are scheduled or underway.

Pod install reference

A compact list of every MOD pod with its current location, install date, and status shown as small colored dots (hover to read the status names). Click Enlarge for a full, searchable popup.

Recent activity

A running feed of the latest notes and communications across the network, newest first. Click an item to go to the community or sensor it's about.

4. Working with Sensors

The All Sensors page lists every sensor. Search by sensor ID, SOA tag, or community name, filter by type, and click any sensor to open its page. Each row has a + Log button for logging something about that pod.

Active Quants, Retired Quants, and Other LCS

  • Active Quants: the QuantAQ MOD pods currently in use (the default view).
  • Retired Quants: MOD pods pulled out of service. Their full history is kept and they can be restored.
  • Other LCS: other low-cost sensors that aren't QuantAQ MODs (for example AQMesh units).

To add a sensor, click + Add Sensor. A toggle at the top of the form chooses Quant (MOD) or Other LCS; then fill in the details.

The sensor page

The top of a sensor's page shows its Type, Status, Community, Address/Coordinates, SOA Tag ID, Purchase Date, and a Notes / Details box (click ✎ Edit to change it). Type, status, and location are changed through + New Log (see Section 6). The tabs are:

  • Log History: notes, status changes, moves, and communications for this pod, with a type filter and keyword search. Service, audit, and collocation activity is kept on their own tabs instead.
  • Collocations: every collocation this pod was part of, including its initial collocation from the Salesforce data.
  • Audits: audits involving this pod.
  • Service Tickets: open and closed tickets with their progress notes, and a + New Service Ticket button.

Retiring and restoring a sensor

On a sensor's page, click Retire Sensor. You can add an optional reason (type @ to tag a contact); it's logged in the sensor's history along with what its status was. The pod's status becomes Retired, it moves to the Retired Quants tab, and its current install in the community is closed. It isn't deleted.

Choosing Retired in a status list (in a + New Log row it's in the Other… menu) does exactly the same thing: it opens the same Retire popup. To bring a sensor back, open it from the Retired Quants tab and click Restore from archive. Its status goes back to Offline; update it with + New Log.

Sensor statuses

A sensor can carry several statuses at once (for example "Online" plus "SD Card Issue"). Change a sensor's status with + New Log: add the sensor, then click its status buttons and say why in the note (see Section 6). Service tickets, audits, and collocations also update statuses automatically. Admins can edit statuses directly in Setup Mode.

Changing the status list (admins)

Admins can add and remove statuses. Click ✎ Edit status list wherever a status list appears (in a New Log row it's in the Other… menu), on the All Sensors page, or in Settings under Sensor Statuses.

  • Add: type the name and click Add. Tick "It's a problem" if sensors with it should appear under Sensors needing attention.
  • Remove: click Remove. The app tells you how many sensors have that status, then takes it off them (leaving that status blank) and notes it in each sensor's history. A second box then lets you choose a new status for each of those sensors, or retire them. You can also leave them as they are.
  • Statuses marked 🔒 can't be removed because the app sets them itself: Online, Offline, Lab Storage, Needs Repair, Retired, and the ones the workflows manage.

Non-admins see the same list; ask an admin if a status needs adding or removing.

Statuses you set

  • Online: deployed and reporting data.
  • Offline: not reporting data.
  • Lost Connection: the pod has stopped reporting.
  • Lab Storage: at a lab, not currently deployed.
  • Needs Repair: has a known problem and shouldn't be deployed until it's fixed.
  • PM Sensor Issue: the particulate matter sensor (OPC or nephelometer) is malfunctioning.
  • Gaseous Sensor Issue: one or more gas sensors (CO, NO, NO2, O3) is malfunctioning.
  • SD Card Issue: the pod's microSD card is faulting.
  • Possible Auto Shutoff Firmware Issue: the pod may be shutting itself off because of a firmware bug.
  • Retired: the pod is out of service (see Retiring above).
  • Plus any statuses your admins have added.

Statuses the workflows set

These are added and removed automatically by service tickets, audits, and collocations. You can pick them by hand, but it's better to let the workflows manage them so the history and dates stay in sync.

  • Collocation: the pod is in an active collocation.
  • Auditing a Community: the pod is the audit pod in an audit that's in progress.
  • Audit: [stage] (for example "Audit: In Progress"): the community pod being audited.
  • Quant Ticket in Progress: an open service ticket exists for the pod. Clicking the tag opens the ticket.
  • Shipped to Quant, Service at Quant, Shipped from Quant: where the pod is in its service ticket. When the ticket reaches Received, the pod is set to Offline.

Moving or swapping a sensor

To move a pod, open + New Log, add the pod, and choose the new community in its Move to row. The note text and install date are filled in for you. The Shortcuts at the top of the row do common changes in one click: Bring online, Send to lab (Offline + Lab Storage, moved to the lab), and Flag for repair.

To swap pods at a site, open More changes on the outgoing pod's row and click ↔ Replacing this pod? Add the incoming replacement. The outgoing pod is set to Offline + Lab Storage and sent to the lab, and a "Replacement sensor" row appears for you to pick the incoming pod. One log records both moves, in the history of both pods and both communities.

5. Communities

Communities are listed alphabetically, with sub-communities nested under their parent. Use the Active and Inactive tabs and the tag filter at the top.

The community page

The toolbar at the top has ✎ (rename), + Edit Tags (pick tags or add your own), ☆ Pin, + Sub-Community, Change Parent, and Deactivate / Reactivate. The tabs are:

  • Overview
  • Sensors, with the community's install history
  • Contacts
  • Log History: notes and communications, filterable (All / Communications only / Notes & activity only) with keyword search
  • Files: + Upload File for images, PDFs, Word, and Excel files
  • Audits
How Community History Works
A community's history only shows notes explicitly tagged to that community. If a sensor moves in, you'll see the move (and future notes tagged to the community), not the sensor's whole history from previous communities. That's on the sensor's own page.
Deactivating a Community
Deactivating a community also marks its active contacts inactive. Reactivating the community does not reactivate those contacts; do that on each contact. A deactivated community shows a 🗑 in the list that deletes it permanently, so only use it for communities added by mistake.

6. Logging & History

Everything that happens to a sensor or community is recorded with + New Log, which is on the dashboard, the sensors list, and every sensor, community, and contact page. It's the one place to change a sensor's status or community, so the change and the reason are always saved together.

Quickest way to change a status
Open the sensor's page and click + New Log. The pod is already added and open. Click the status buttons to turn statuses on or off, write a short note saying why, and click Save Log.
  1. Which sensors are involved? Search a sensor ID, or a community name to add all its pods. Each pod becomes a row; click a row to open it (with just one pod, it opens by itself).
    • Status: every status is a button. Click to turn it on or off; a pod can have more than one. Statuses being added show with a + and a gold outline, and ones being removed are crossed out. Online and Offline switch each other off. Less common statuses, Retired, and (for admins) editing the list are in Other….
    • Move to: pick the community the pod is going to.
    • Shortcuts: Bring online, Send to lab, Flag for repair.
    • More changes: the pod's type and mounting location, and swapping in a replacement pod.
    • With two or more pods, Same change for all pods adds one status or move to every pod at once.
    Skip this step for a note that isn't about a sensor. Closed rows show what's changing, so you can check everything at a glance.
  2. What happened, and why? Write a short note (type @ to tag a contact), check the date, and if it was a call, email, or site visit, pick Call, Email, or Site Visit. The sensors' communities are tagged automatically; tag others if needed.

The This will be saved as box at the bottom shows exactly what will be recorded. Click Save Log. It's saved as one note in the history of each sensor, community, and tagged contact. A call, email, or site visit with no sensors is saved as a communication.

Logging a sensor issue

Open + New Log, add the pod, and click the issue status (PM Sensor Issue, Gaseous Sensor Issue, SD Card Issue, Lost Connection, Needs Repair, or the firmware issue), or the Flag for repair shortcut. Describe what's wrong in the note. The pod then appears under "Sensors needing attention" on the dashboard.

Editing a log

Click ✎ on any entry in a history.

  • Within an hour of saving a sensor log (if nothing else has changed on those pods since), the full form reopens and you can fix anything, including the status change or move itself.
  • After that, the form opens at the pods' current state. You can fix the words, date, and tags, and any new sensor changes you make are added on; nothing earlier is undone.
  • Plain notes open as Edit Note, and communications as Edit Communication.

Adding a follow-up to a note

Each note has an Add Note button that adds a follow-up underneath it, stamped with your name and the date. Type @ to tag a contact; the note then shows on their contact page too.

Deleting a log

Click the trash icon. If the log changed any sensors, the confirm box lists them under "Also undo this log's sensor changes" (ticked), so deleting the log also puts each pod's status, community, type, and location back the way they were, for any number of pods. A pod that's been changed again since that log is listed separately and left alone, so later changes aren't lost. Deleted logs go to the Trash Bin for 30 days (see Section 15).

History filters

On a sensor page, the Log History dropdown shows All History, Issues, Sensor Movement, Status Changes, Communications, or General Notes. Community pages filter by All / Communications only / Notes & activity only, and contact pages by All / Communications / Info Updates. Every history has a keyword search box.

7. Contacts & Email

Contacts are anyone involved in the network: tribal administrators, school staff, facility managers, partners, and so on. The Contacts page has three tabs:

  • Active Communities: contacts grouped by community. Inactive people stay listed under their community with an "Inactive" badge, at the bottom.
  • Non-Community: partners, vendors, and coordinators not tied to one community.
  • Inactive Communities: contacts of deactivated communities.

A contact can belong to several communities. Each row has a Mass Email List checkbox, and a contact can be marked as a community's Primary Contact. On a contact's page, click any field to edit it. The page has + New Log, a history (filter: All / Communications / Info Updates), and keyword search. Clicking a contact's email address starts an email to them.

Inactive, not deleted
When someone is no longer involved, set them to Inactive. Their history is kept and they can be made active again. Deleting a contact (🗑) is permanent and does not go to the Trash Bin.

Emailing contacts

  1. On the Contacts page, click ✉ Email.
  2. Choose recipients in any combination:
    • Add a person: type a name (or email) and pick them from the list that appears.
    • Mass Email List selects everyone on the list.
    • Filter by Community shows and selects one community's contacts.
    • Tick or untick people in the list, or use Select All / Deselect All.
    The count next to the search box shows how many people are selected.
  3. BCC all recipients is on by default, so people don't see each other's addresses.
  4. Write the subject and body, then click Draft & Send in Outlook. Outlook opens with the email ready; review it and send it there.
  5. Back in the app, the Log Email Communication? box appears. Click Log Communication to record the email in each recipient's and community's history.

Opening a ticket

Click + New Ticket (Quant Service Tickets page), + Service Ticket (dashboard), or + New Service Ticket (a sensor's Service Tickets tab). Pick the sensor(s); hold Ctrl (Cmd on a Mac) to pick several. Tick Issue / Repair and/or Calibration, describe the issue, and add the RMA number if you have it (the ticket then starts at "RMA Assigned").

Moving a ticket along

Open the ticket and click Advance to: [next stage]. The Undo link steps back if you advanced by mistake. The sensors' statuses update automatically at each stage (see Section 4).

Multi-sensor tickets

One ticket can cover several sensors shipped together; they all advance as one. Each sensor's Service Tickets tab links to the shared ticket.

Progress notes & tracking

Each ticket has a Progress Notes section for timestamped updates. They show on the ticket and on each pod's Service Tickets tab, and on the page of any contact you @-tag. There are two tracking fields: Tracking Info (to QuantAQ) and Return Tracking Info (from QuantAQ). Enter the carrier and number, like "UPS, 1234567890".

Closing a ticket

Click Close Out. Fill in the Work Completed Summary and choose Set Sensor Status To (if you don't choose, the pod is set to Online). The ticket's service statuses are removed from the pod. Closed tickets are listed at the bottom of the Quant Service Tickets page. A type filter at the top shows All, Issue/Repair, or Calibration tickets.

9. Collocations

A collocation places community sensors at a regulatory site (Garden, NCore, or Floyd Dryden) next to a BAM reference monitor and/or a permanent pod to check that the sensors read accurately.

Starting a collocation

On the Collocations page, click + Start Collocation and fill in:

  • Location: regulatory sites are listed first, then communities A to Z.
  • Sensors: the pods being collocated.
  • Start Date and End Date (tick TBD if the end isn't known yet).
  • Conducted By (defaults to you) and any Notes.

The pods get the "Collocation" status. It's removed when you advance to Finished, Analysis Pending (and put back if you undo).

Scheduling an audit

On the Audits page, click + Schedule Audit and fill in the Audit Pod, Community, Community Pod, Scheduled Start and End, Install Team, Takedown Team, and any notes.

During the audit

  • Advancing to In Progress sets the audit pod to Online + Auditing a Community, and tags the community pod "Audit: In Progress".
  • When the audit pod is picked up, click Advance to: Finished, Analysis Pending. A box asks what the audit pod is doing now (Lab Storage, checked by default, Offline, or Needs Repair) and lets you add a note, using @ to tag whoever shipped or received it. Click Advance Audit. The note is saved to the audit's progress notes, the audit pod's history, and each tagged contact's page.

The audit popup

Click an audit to open it. You can edit the Start and End Dates and the teams, add progress notes, Upload Photos, see the Analysis Results table, Begin Analysis / View Analysis / Re-upload Data / Delete Dataset (see Section 11), preview or download the Excel file, and Delete Audit.

Other audit tools

  • Upload Audit from Excel: creates a finished audit from a completed audit spreadsheet. The pods and dates fill in from the file name, you pick the sheet, and the Excel is attached. These show as "Complete, Excel Analysis".
  • All Audit Results: pass/fail for every audit, grouped by community, with Save Printable HTML.

11. Data Analysis & Reports

Once a collocation or audit is at Finished, Analysis Pending, click Upload Data (collocations) or Begin Analysis (audits) and choose the Excel file. The app runs the full regression analysis for you. For audits, if every parameter passes, the audit moves to Complete automatically.

Collocations: preparing the data

  1. Export hourly data from AirVision for the BAM, the permanent pod, and all community pods.
  2. For regulatory site data, use PM2.5 and PM10 local conditions.
  3. Clean up any invalidated data and trim to the collocation dates.
  4. Don't remove the first 24 hours: the app handles that automatically.
  5. Choose the BAM source (Garden, NCore, or Floyd Dryden BAM) and, optionally, the permanent pod. Without a permanent pod, every Quant is compared with every other Quant. All Quants are compared with the BAM either way.
  6. Upload the Excel file.

Audits: preparing the data

Audits use one AirVision hourly file with the audit pod's and community pod's columns (no BAM or permanent pod). The analysis has two tabs, Analysis and Raw Data, and Generate Report saves the report.

What the app does

  • Reads all sensor columns from the spreadsheet.
  • Leaves the first 24 hours out of the regression (the settling-in period).
  • Runs linear regression for each parameter (PM2.5, PM10, CO, NO, NO2, O3).
  • Checks the DQI thresholds: R² ≥ 0.70, slope 0.65 to 1.35, intercept ±5, SD ≤ 5, and RMSE ≤ 7. All five must pass.
  • Draws time series charts and regression scatter plots.
  • Runs validation checks to confirm the math (see below).

Collocation analysis tabs

  • Time Series: hourly plots for each parameter. Click a sensor in the legend to hide or show it.
  • Pods vs BAM: each sensor compared with the BAM (PM only).
  • Quants PM: each pod compared with the permanent pod for PM (or with each other pod if there's no permanent pod).
  • Quants Gaseous: the same for CO, NO, NO2, and O3.
  • Data Sheet: the full data table, with the first 24 hours shaded yellow and missing or removed data in red.

Click Save as HTML to download a standalone report that opens in any browser with all the interactive charts. The file name looks like Garden_Collocation_Analysis_445_451_674_Mar19_Apr1_2026.html.

Reading the reports

  • Pass/fail colors: each value is shown in a green box when it passes its DQI threshold and a red box when it fails.
  • Dates: reports use the audit's Start Date and End Date, so correct those in the audit popup if the real dates differed from the schedule.
  • Progress notes in the report show when each was logged.

Validation checks

Every analysis includes automatic checks (open the validation dropdown to review them):

  • Self-test: confirms the regression engine gives the right answers on known test data.
  • R² cross-check: recalculates each R² a second way (Pearson correlation) and flags any mismatch.
  • Timestamp validation: checks for data gaps, short datasets, and date problems.
  • Sample size: flags when there isn't enough data for reliable results.
  • Borderline DQI: warns when a pass/fail result is very close to the threshold.
Good to Know
X-PM sensors (the Modulair-PM models) only compare PM2.5 and PM10. Gas parameters are skipped automatically since they don't measure gases. Re-upload Data clears the old results for good, so only use it when you mean to start over.

12. Exporting Data

Sensors, Communities, and Contacts each have an Export as Spreadsheet button at the top of the page.

  1. Click Export as Spreadsheet.
  2. Tick or untick the fields you want. Every field is on by default.
  3. For Contacts and Communities, you can also include inactive records (active only is the default).
  4. Click Download. An .xlsx file named for the list and today's date downloads.
  • Sensors (active sensors only): ID, type, status, community, location, install date, SOA tag, purchase date, and any custom fields added in Setup Mode.
  • Communities: name, parent, tags, status, sensor count and IDs, sub-community count, and active contact count. Counts are for that community only, not its sub-communities.
  • Contacts: name, role, community, organization, email, phone, Mass Email List (Yes/No), and status.

13. Settings & User Management

Click the gear icon next to your name at the top of the sidebar. Everyone can manage their own password and authenticator here (see Section 2). The rest of this section is for administrators.

Users

User Management lists everyone with access, with an Admin badge on administrators. On other people's rows, admins can:

  • Change the role between User and Admin. Keep at least two administrators, so someone can always reset passwords or authenticators. The app warns you if there's only one.
  • Reset password: shows a temporary password once. Copy it before closing the box and give it to the person by phone or Teams, not email. They choose a new password at their next sign-in.
  • Reset MFA: removes their authenticator; they set it up again at their next sign-in. If someone lost both their password and their phone, use both buttons.
  • Guide Editor: lets that person edit this User Guide (see Editing This Guide below).
  • Archive: see below.

Inviting someone

Click + Invite New User, enter their email, and choose User or Admin. Your email program opens with an invitation to send them (if it doesn't, click Open Email). Until they create their account, they're listed under Pending Invites, where Cancel invite takes them off the approved list.

When someone leaves: Archive

Click Archive on their row. They lose access immediately: they can't sign in or see any data. Everything they entered is kept: their notes, communications, audits, and edits stay in the app under their name. Users are never deleted. You can't archive yourself; another admin has to do it.

Bringing someone back: Reactivate

Archived people are listed under Archived Users. Click Reactivate. They sign in with their same email and password. The box has "Also reset their authenticator (MFA)", ticked by default: they'll set up their authenticator again at their next sign-in, which helps if they have a new phone. If they've forgotten their password, click Reset password on their row afterward.

Other settings

  • Multi-Factor Authentication: whether everyone must use an authenticator code. Leave it on.
  • Sensor Statuses: add or remove sensor statuses (see Section 4).
  • Trash Bin: see Section 15.

Editing This Guide

Anyone an admin has given Guide Editor access (the checkbox on their row in User Management) can update this guide. When a feature changes, please update the guide so new staff learn the current way of doing things.

  1. Open User Guide (bottom of the sidebar) and click Edit at the top. Guide Editors are the only ones who see this button.
  2. The whole guide opens as text with formatting codes (HTML). Find the part you want to change (Ctrl+F helps) and edit the words between the codes. For example, in <p>Click <strong>Save</strong>.</p>, change only "Click" and "Save" and leave the <p> and <strong> parts alone. To add a paragraph, copy an existing <p>…</p> line and change its words.
  3. Click Preview to see the result in a new tab. If the layout looks broken, a code was probably deleted by accident.
  4. Click Save & Publish. The change is live for everyone right away, and the top of the guide shows who last edited it and when. Cancel throws your changes away.
  • The "Last updated" date on the cover and at the bottom is filled in automatically when you save, so you don't need to change it.
  • Your typing is auto-saved in your browser while you work. If the browser closes before you save, click Edit again and the app offers to restore your draft.
  • Before a big edit, click Export as HTML to keep a copy of the current guide, just in case.
For whoever maintains the app
The guide also exists as the file user-guide.html in the GitHub repository. Once someone saves an edit here, the app shows that saved copy instead of the file, so later changes to the file won't appear until the saved copy is cleared (in the database, rename or remove the app_settings row with key user_guide_body). To keep the two in step, use Export as HTML after editing here and save the result over user-guide.html.

14. Admin Modes (Setup & Sandbox)

Admins see two toggles at the bottom of the sidebar: Setup Mode and Sandbox Mode. They do very different things.

Setup Mode

Setup Mode is for bulk data entry and backfilling, when you're correcting or adding many records and don't want an automatic history note for every edit. While it's on:

  • Edits save to the real database as usual. This is not a practice mode.
  • The app stops writing automatic history notes for status changes, moves, contact edits, and so on.
  • The date and note prompts on edits are skipped.
  • Extra controls appear: add custom sensor fields, edit sensor IDs and statuses directly, restore hidden columns, a Move button on sensor rows, and Delete Sensor on sensor pages (permanent; use Retire Sensor instead unless it was added by mistake).

Turn it off when you're done. It also turns off when you sign out or close the browser.

Heads Up
If you forget to turn Setup Mode off, your day-to-day edits stop writing history notes. Watch for the amber "Setup Mode ON" pill in the sidebar.

Sandbox Mode

Sandbox Mode is a safe practice space. You see the real, current data, but nothing you change is saved: it only lives in your browser. Use it to try a workflow or show someone around. A bright amber banner stays at the top the whole time.

  • Refreshing the page throws away your practice changes but keeps you in Sandbox.
  • To leave, click Exit sandbox or sign out.
  • Password and authenticator resets are turned off in Sandbox.
Which One Do I Want?
Practicing, exploring, or training? Sandbox Mode. Nothing is saved.
Doing a lot of real cleanup and want to skip the auto-notes? Setup Mode. Everything is saved.

15. Trash Bin, Archived & Deleted Items

Deleted notes, communications, service tickets, audits, and collocations go to the Trash Bin (Settings → Trash Bin, admins only). Tickets, audits, and collocations are deleted with the Delete button at the bottom of their popup; that record's automatic history notes go to the Trash Bin with it (only its own, not those of other tickets, audits, or collocations on the same pods).

  • Restore puts the item back where it came from. Refresh the page afterward to see it in place.
  • Purge deletes it for good.
  • Items are purged automatically 30 days after they were deleted.
These are permanent (no Trash Bin)
Deleting a contact, a community, a file, or a sensor (Delete Sensor in Setup Mode) can't be undone. Prefer Inactive (contacts), Deactivate (communities), and Retire Sensor (sensors), which keep the history.

Retired sensors are on the Retired Quants tab of All Sensors and in the Trash Bin's archived-sensors filter. They're never purged automatically, because tickets, audits, and notes still refer to them. Archived users are in Settings (see Section 13).

16. Tips & Good-to-Knows

Timezones
Everything in the app runs on Alaska time (America/Anchorage).
Online & Offline
These two statuses can't both be on. Choosing Online removes Offline, and vice versa.
One Log, Not Two
When you write a note and change a sensor in the same log, it's saved as one entry: the change is added to your note.
Annotating Quick Edits
When you edit a field directly on a page (for example a sensor's Purchase Date) or change a contact's status, a small box asks for a date and a note so the change is recorded in the history. Fill it in, or click Skip.
Saving This Guide
Use Export as HTML at the top of the User Guide page to save a copy you can open offline or share. To change the guide, see Editing This Guide.
Imported Salesforce Records
Notes brought over from Salesforce show "Created by [name], SF import". The tool used to bring them over is described in the repository at tools/sf-transfer/.
